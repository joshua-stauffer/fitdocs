## What

Task 7.2 needs four spec files, one of which is the `--replace-text` rule set.
Generating it by hand took five passes; each pass satisfied one invariant and
broke another. That is the oscillation signature, and it was happening on the
one artifact in this spec that cannot be corrected after it runs.

The rules must be produced by a module with tests, the same way every other
one-shot in this spec is.

## The mechanical finding this is really about

**`--replace-text` cannot preserve case.** `build_replacement_expressions`
hard-codes an inline `(?i)`. Under that flag a `The <token>` rule also matches
`the <token>`, so whichever rule is listed first wins for *both* cases: you
either capitalise ordinary lowercase prose or lowercase sentence starts.
Measured on the real corpus: **36 sentence-start lowercasings**.

Preserving capitalisation therefore requires **case-sensitive** patterns
(`regex:` lines with no `(?i)`) plus one rule per observed case variant of each
token. That is a different generator from the one `scripts/purge/rewrite.py`
currently offers, and it is a deliberate departure from
`build_replacement_expressions` that should be recorded rather than improvised.

## The six invariants the tests must pin

Every one is measurable against the real history, and all six must hold at once
— that is the part hand-editing kept failing.

1. Zero identifying tokens left in surviving blob content.
2. Zero identifying tokens left in commit messages.
3. Zero identity leaks: the third party's given name, their email address, the
   maintainer's mailbox in blob content, and the real machine hostname.
4. Zero mangled forms (`withdrawnwithdrawn`, `the the`, `a the`, `an_withdrawn`,
   `Withdrawned`, and the email-domain fragment).
5. Zero sentence-start lowercasings.
6. **The tip no-op invariant**: applying the rules to every blob at `HEAD`
   changes nothing. This holds only because task 6.3 emptied the tip, and it is
   the cheapest available proof that the rules are scoped correctly.

## Four traps already paid for

- **The given name is an ordinary English word.** Unguarded it matched inside
  "Markdown" across **292** tip blobs. Even word-boundary-guarded it matches the
  standalone English verb throughout `.claude/skills/`. It has exactly **two**
  real uses in all of history, and both are line-wrapped away from the surname,
  so the full-name rule must tolerate whitespace and the bare given name must
  never get a rule of its own — target the two exact phrases.
- **One `.local` hostname in the corpus is a synthetic test fixture** in
  `tests/purge/test_sweep.py`, not a real machine. Redacting it rewrites a
  fixture and breaks the tip no-op invariant.
- **Tokens appear adjectivally** ("a `<token>` symbol"), where the replacement
  must be "a withdrawn symbol", not "a the withdrawn methodology symbol".
- **A shadowed duplicate rule is not safe to delete.** Removing some as dead
  destroyed capitalisation handling, because under `(?i)` they were not
  duplicates at all — they were the only case-preserving rules.

## Evidence

Measured at `3cfe28e`. The case-insensitive rule set satisfied invariants 1, 2,
3, 4 and 6 but failed 5 with 36 lowercasings. The case-sensitive rewrite
satisfied 3 and 6 but regressed 1 (9 tokens surviving) and 4, because it missed
observed case variants and carried a bug in the bare-token replacement.

Artifacts left in `~/.fitdocs-purge/`, none of them approved and `rules.json`
known incorrect: `rules.json`, `plan-content.tsv`, `plan-paths.tsv`,
`forms.json`, `rename-targets.json`, `replacements-proposed.md`.

## How to pick it up

Build the generator as a module under `scripts/purge/`, inside the pytest and
mypy perimeter, with the six invariants as tests running against the real
history. Then regenerate, render the table masked, and take it to the
maintainer for approval before any invocation.

Do not hand-edit `rules.json`. That is precisely what oscillated.

Done when all six invariants hold simultaneously, proven by tests rather than
by a script run, and the maintainer has approved the rendered table.
