# Brief: load-history

## Problem

fitdocs computes a training load per activity and stops. Planning the next
block needs the other axis: how load has accumulated and decayed over months
and years — the fitness, fatigue and form curves every platform draws and the
athlete currently has nowhere. The archive holds 2,478 pages spanning 2017 to
2026; the question "what did my fitness look like going into that marathon,
and what did the twelve weeks before it look like" has no answer inside the
wiki today.

The roadmap deferred "weekly load aggregation views" with the plan level from
the first pass onward (roadmap `## Scope`, Phase 4 `#### Scope`;
product.md "Explicitly deferred"). Phase 6 lifts that one deferral. Cycles and
blocks — documents that *prescribe* training — stay deferred; this page only
describes what happened.

## Current State

- Every page that has had a load pass carries `load_value`,
  `load_methodology` and `load_basis` in its frontmatter (`LOAD_KEYS`,
  `src/fitdocs/contract.py:269-290`) plus a `<!-- fitdocs-load:v2 {json} -->`
  payload line with the non-selected channel values, flags, inputs and notes
  (`src/fitdocs/load/render.py`). The page's local date is readable through
  `contract.document_date`, and `fitdocs.docio.read_frontmatter` is the one
  shared reader. So a complete daily load series is readable **from the
  documents alone, without touching a `.fit` file**.
- Nothing aggregates. `threshold-load` Req 11.6 forbids the calculator from
  aggregating over time (correctly — that is a different decision from
  scoring one activity), and `training-load` lists "weekly/cycle load
  aggregation" out of scope.
- The fitness–fatigue model is already extracted from both defining texts in
  `docs/reference/banister-trimp-primary-sources.md` §3: Morton, Fitz-Clarke &
  Banister (1990) eq. (4), (5), (8); Banister (1991) pp. 413–415. The document
  is explicit (D5) that the 45-day / 15-day constants are *illustrative*
  starting values, not fitted results, and (D6) that even the source's fitted
  model predicted peaking imperfectly.
- The data root has **no home for a page that is not one activity**
  (`src/fitdocs/layout.py`: `doc_path` is always `workouts/<stem>.md`, and
  anything under `workouts/` that is not `type: workout` is skipped). Owned
  paths, declared directories and the confinement guard
  (`tests/test_confinement.py`) all assume per-activity output.
- The chart layer has a generic x/y SVG line renderer
  (`src/fitdocs/render/charts/hero.py`) but it is per-activity by
  construction: km/min x-axis only, band-normalised series, y ticks only for a
  single series. `charts/svg.py` and `charts/series.py` are reusable
  primitives.
- On the athlete's archive today nearly every page reads *not computed*,
  because prompt-answered benchmarks are dated today and never apply to the
  past (queue item `2026-08-27-prompt-date-strands-historical-documents`,
  critical, maintainer-accepted for follow-up). Phase 6 lands that fix first
  (wave 0) and derives dated benchmarks from tagged races
  (`performance-benchmarks`); this spec reads whatever loads exist and says
  honestly where they do not.

## Desired Outcome

- **One page in the data root** — the training-load history — regenerated by
  a `fitdocs history` command and, if the design finds it cheap, at the end of
  every load pass. It carries:
  - an SVG chart of **fitness, fatigue and form** over the full archive on a
    calendar axis, with each tagged race marked and its result shown
    (`effort-tags`);
  - a **weekly table**: week, total load, session count, fitness/fatigue/form
    at week end, and how many of that week's activities carry a computed load;
  - a **coverage statement** per period, because a stretch of *not computed*
    pages reads as rest on the curve, which is a lie the page must not tell.
    Where coverage is poor the curve is suppressed or visibly annotated; the
    threshold is configuration and the default is cited or measured, never
    assumed.
- The model is the exponentially-weighted recursion of Morton et al. eq. (4)
  and (5), in the daily-average form every platform draws (Coggan's CTL/ATL,
  "Fitness/Fatigue" on intervals.icu — the Phase 4 interop target). Form is
  fitness minus fatigue. The time constants are **configuration with cited
  seed defaults**, and the page states which constants produced the curve and
  that they are seeds, not fitted values (D5). `performance-model-fit` is the
  spec that replaces seeds with the athlete's own fit.
- The series sums **one methodology**: the page names the calculator whose
  loads it summed and excludes pages scored by another, since loads on
  different scales do not add. The default is the configured default
  calculator.
- Byte-identical output for an unchanged data root. The chart's span is the
  archive's span; the page reads no clock except the pass's single resolved
  `today`, and only for an "as of" line if the design keeps one.

## Approach

A new top-level package that reads only through the shipped document readers
(`docio.read_frontmatter`, `contract.document_date`, `LOAD_KEYS`, the payload
parser, and `effort-tags`' reader), builds a daily series, runs the recursion,
and renders one markdown page plus one SVG next to it. It never parses a
`.fit`, never reads `athlete.toml` for anything but the configured calculator
name, and never writes a workout page.

The page lives at a new owned location declared in `layout.py` and added to the
ownership contract and the confinement guard in the same change — the design
decides between a top-level `history/` directory and a sibling of `workouts/`.
A new chart module under `render/charts/` gives the calendar axis and
absolute-scaled multi-series plot the hero chart deliberately does not have,
built on the same SVG primitives.

## Scope

- **In**: the daily load series from documents; the fitness/fatigue/form
  recursion with configurable, cited seed constants; race markers from effort
  tags; the weekly table; the coverage statement and suppression rule; the
  page, its SVG, and its owned location; the `fitdocs history` command; the
  settings keys it needs.
- **Out**: fitting the constants to the athlete's results
  (`performance-model-fit`); forecasting form at a future date; plan, cycle or
  block documents; per-activity back-links from workout pages to the history
  page (a listed candidate, not a deliverable); per-sport or per-channel
  split curves (candidate); any change to how a single activity is scored;
  reading `.fit` files.

## Boundary Candidates

- **Series assembly** (pure): documents → `(date, load, methodology,
  computed?)` rows → a daily series over the archive's span, with the
  coverage measure alongside.
- **The model** (pure): the recursion, its constants as `CitedConstant`
  records in a layer `sources.py`, and the form definition. Testable against
  Morton et al.'s worked figures (day-60 fitness 3,351 / fatigue 3,044 for
  T = 100, τ₁ = 45, τ₂ = 15) — the one set of published numbers that *are*
  usable as vectors, unlike Banister's figure captions (queue item
  `2026-07-27-banister-figure-captions-unusable-as-vectors`).
- **Rendering**: the calendar-axis chart module and the page template.
- **The pass and its location**: command, owned path, confinement, settings.

## Out of Boundary

- What a given fitness or form value *means* for the athlete — no zones, no
  "ready to race" verdicts, no overload guardrails.
- Any load value. The page reads them; it never computes or alters one.
- The effort-tag vocabulary (`effort-tags`) and derived thresholds
  (`performance-benchmarks`).

## Upstream / Downstream

- **Upstream**: `training-load` (the load keys, payload and pass — and the
  wave-0 prompt-date fix that makes the archive's loads exist);
  `wiki-contract` (a new owned location and, if the page is typed, a new
  document type); `effort-tags` (race markers); `threshold-load` only as the
  producer of the loads summed; `render/charts` primitives.
- **Downstream**: `performance-model-fit` consumes this spec's daily series and
  replaces its seed constants with fitted ones; a future forecast feature
  would extend the page.

## Existing Spec Touchpoints

- **Extends**: `training-load` — its out-of-scope line deferring aggregation
  is amended to point here; `wiki-contract` — owned paths and the ownership
  documentation gain the history page.
- **Adjacent**: `activity-qa-flags` (flags on a page might one day annotate a
  day on the curve — not now); `distribution` (a new command is a README
  line).

## Constraints

- **Every constant is a `CitedConstant`.** The 45/15-day seeds cite Banister
  (1991) pp. 413–414 and are labelled illustrative per D5. The 42/7-day pair
  of Coggan's Performance Management Chart — the interop default — has **no
  peer-reviewed origin**: the viability check (2026-09-09) traced it to
  Coggan's 2006 Cycling Summit presentation, his TrainingPeaks article "The
  Science of the Performance Manager" (which calls them "nominal values based
  on the scientific literature" and drops k₁/k₂ from the model), and Allen &
  Coggan, *Training and Racing with a Power Meter* 2nd ed. (2010), chapter 8
  by reviewer account with the page unverified. It lands as secondary
  attestation, and the page says so. The recursion form is a fitdocs choice
  to record: TrainingPeaks' help text uses the `1/τ` approximation
  (`CTL += (TSS − CTL)/42`) while Morton et al. eq. (4) is the exact
  `e^(−1/τ)` decay; the two differ by about 1% per step and fitdocs must pick
  one and justify it. No bare numeric literal in the model module; the
  existing literal-guard pattern applies.
- **Honesty about gaps is a requirement, not a rendering nicety.** A day with
  no page inside the archive's span is a genuine zero; a page marked *not
  computed* is missing data, and the two must never look the same on the
  chart or in the table. Before the archive's first page the series is absent,
  not zero.
- **Documents are the only input.** No `.fit` parsing, no network, no clock
  beyond the pass's resolved `today`. This is what keeps the pass fast on a
  2,478-page wiki and byte-identical between runs.
- **One methodology per curve.** Mixed-calculator archives get a stated
  exclusion, not a silent sum.
- The new owned location must be added to `layout.OWNED_PATHS` /
  `DECLARED_DIRS`, the ownership contract, and `tests/test_confinement.py`'s
  permitted set in one change, or the confinement guard reds.
- Fixtures are synthetic pages; the athlete's real wiki is a manual check,
  never a committed fixture.
